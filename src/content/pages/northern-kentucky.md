## Close enough to come back

Fort Mitchell, Fort Thomas, Villa Hills, Crestview Hills, Edgewood, and Union are all short drives, and that changes what is practical. If a panel needs dressing again a week after it hangs, I dress it again. I can drop a memo off to sit in your room overnight so you see it in the morning light instead of deciding on the spot. Being close is not really a selling point. It is the reason the finishing actually happens.

## Drapery first, then everything around it

Drapery is the largest piece of fabric in most rooms and it sets the terms for the rest. Where the rod goes, how far the panels stack back, what the lining does to the light. Once that is settled, blinds and shades, upholstery, and the pillows and cushions can be worked around it rather than negotiated against it afterward.

Fabric comes from the lines I represent, chosen for how it will hang in your specific room rather than how it reads in a book.

## River-view rooms and new builds

A Fort Thomas room facing the river has a view worth keeping and afternoon light worth controlling, which are competing requirements, and it is the reason openness factor is a real number rather than a preference. A new build in Union has square openings and no history, which makes the work easier and makes the fabric decision carry more of the weight.
